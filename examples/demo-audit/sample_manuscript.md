# Abstract

We analysed six years of monitoring data (January 2018–December 2022) from a network of 33 wells across the Demo Alluvial Fan. A per-well single-tank model reproduced seasonal groundwater levels with a median Kling–Gupta efficiency of 0.63. Coastal water levels were validated against tidal signals, which showed a strong correlation, confirming the model's physical realism. We conclude that the approach quantifies groundwater–surface-water exchange across the fan.

# 1. Introduction

Alluvial-fan aquifers are difficult to characterise because recharge and discharge vary sharply between the inland apex and the coastal margin. The objective of this study is to quantify recharge rates across the Demo Alluvial Fan using a parsimonious water-balance model calibrated to seasonal observations.

# 2. Methods

We instrumented 33 monitoring wells, classified as inland or coastal. For each well we fitted a single-tank water-balance model in which the recession coefficient controls baseflow depletion. Parameters were estimated by differential evolution and selected by validation Kling–Gupta efficiency (KGE). The recession behaviour is summarised by the depletion constant reported in Table 1.

# 3. Results

Across all 35 wells, the model reproduced the seasonal cycle, with validation KGE ranging from 0.57 to 0.82 (Figure 1). Inland wells outperformed coastal wells (Figure 2). The spatial pattern of the recession coefficient is shown in Figure 3. For the coastal wells, the correlation between modelled levels and the tidal signal was not significant (rho = 0.02, p = 0.94).

# 4. Conclusions

A single-tank model calibrated per well captures seasonal groundwater dynamics across the Demo Alluvial Fan and quantifies groundwater–surface-water exchange at the coastal margin.
